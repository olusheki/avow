## What aVow is

aVow lets you set self-imposed limits — "vows" — that block distracting apps and websites for a period
you choose. You decide what to block and for how long. Once a vow is running you can make it stricter
or add time, but you generally **cannot unlock early**. That's the entire point of the app.

## The lock-out is intentional

Because aVow is built to resist being disabled in a weak moment:

- You may be **unable to open blocked apps, certain settings, or aVow's own controls until a vow
  ends**, or until the app's built-in deactivation waiting period completes.
- **Do not block apps you may need in an emergency** or for anything time-sensitive (for example your
  phone/dialer or messaging). aVow tries to keep the launcher and dialer reachable, but you are
  responsible for what you choose to restrict.
- Set durations you're comfortable being held to. A long vow cannot be shortened.

## Full edition and Device Owner

The full ("aVow Plus") edition can be granted **Device Owner**, an Android administrator role that
makes a vow much harder to escape (it can block uninstalling aVow, factory reset, and Safe Mode).

- Only grant Device Owner on a device you fully control and understand.
- To remove it, use the app's **deactivation flow** (a 24-hour cooling-off period, then confirm),
  which cleanly strips Device Owner so the app can be uninstalled. See the [FAQ](FAQ.md).
- While a vow is active, factory reset and other escape hatches may be blocked by design. Plan
  accordingly before starting a long vow with these protections on.
